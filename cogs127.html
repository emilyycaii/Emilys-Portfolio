COGS 127 Project 🌸

Designing a cultural learning extension for Duolingo to help immigrants communicate more confidently in real-life conversations.

Overview Problem My Role Process Result Reflection

Overview

Duolingo helps users learn grammar and vocabulary, but it does not fully prepare people for real-life conversations. In everyday communication, people often use slang, idioms, abbreviations, and informal expressions that are difficult for new immigrants to understand.

To address this problem, we designed a new Culture section for Duolingo that teaches everyday language, communication styles, and social norms in the United States. The goal is to help users feel more confident and comfortable in real-world situations, not just in classroom-style learning.

This idea was inspired by personal experiences of moving to a new country and struggling to understand casual conversations. We wanted to create a feature that helps people feel more included, connected, and confident in their daily lives.

User Research

We conducted semi-structured interviews with four international students who recently moved to the United States. The interviews focused on their experiences with communication, cultural adjustment, and language-learning apps like Duolingo. We asked participants about challenges in real-life conversations, experiences with slang and small talk, how they learned about American culture, and what kinds of features would help them feel more confident communicating in daily life.

Across all interviews, participants shared similar struggles with real-world communication. Even when they understood English, they often had difficulty understanding slang, jokes, tone, and knowing how to respond naturally in conversations. Many participants felt that language-learning apps only prepare users for basic situations and not real-life interactions. One participant explained that “communication also depends on cultural context, not just language.”

Participants preferred more practical and interactive learning methods, especially role-play exercises, scenario-based conversations, visual examples, and AI conversation practice. Many also shared that real-life experience helped them learn more effectively than traditional language apps.

Another important finding was that participants often stayed quiet when they did not understand conversations because they were afraid of feeling awkward or embarrassed. One participant shared, “I just stay quiet and wait for the conversation to move on.” These responses showed that communication challenges can lead to feelings of uncertainty and isolation.

Overall, our research revealed a gap between formal language learning and real-world communication. Participants wanted tools that not only teach vocabulary and grammar, but also help users understand cultural context, social norms, and everyday communication styles. These insights guided our design process and helped us create a feature that addresses the real needs of language learners in their daily lives.
